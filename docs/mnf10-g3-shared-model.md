## Findings

1. **Arm C on the shipping coder LOSES**: the atlas-ref arm (+479 B) and
   the dedicated shared-template grammar (+880 B) both LOSE to the z19
   container's own LZ layer, which codes the six exact repeats for FREE.
   The residual-atlas fired only 4 of 24 tile refs — the residual block
   keyed on tile-relative residuals whose first column depends on the
   tile's left neighbor, so 20 exact-PIXEL copies have different
   residual blocks (the mechanism boundary: content-equality of residuals
   ≠ content-equality of pixels).
2. **The zstd-disabled control flips the verdict**: under zlib-9's small
   window the same shared-template arm WINS ~6.5 KB (26,354 vs 32,868) —
   the win belongs to a WEAK container's dictionary, not to the atlas
   grammar. Structural modeling vs the strong container's repetition
   machinery: the container wins on this fixture class.
3. **Capability established, practical value bounded**: a shared-model
   grammar CAN represent 6 exact translations for ~30 B of metadata
   (site coords + markers) + one 3,072-B template — but the already-
   present LZ layer does it cheaper inside z19. The practical-value
   question for G3 shifts to classes where the container's dictionary
   cannot reach: streams longer than the z19 window, distributed
   references, transformed (non-identity) repeats (Arm D), and
   corrupted-reference residuals (Arm E — the 25,500-B corrupted fixture
   stands ready).

## Disposition

- Track B's acceptance criterion confirmed as Jon stated it: a shared
  model must beat zstd INCLUDED, on the same bytes, complete cost. On
  the exact-translation fixture: DENIED at z19, PROVEN available under
  a window-constrained container.
- Arms D (transforms) and E (corrupted references) are the next
  measurements; the model family and search budget stay frozen until
  those numbers exist.
- Scope: these three 160x120 fixtures + one coding library per arm;
  photo-corpus generalization only after D/E pass.


## Arm D measured (2026-10-28, aligned-site capability fixture

g3_shared_d8.png: the SAME template at 32-ALIGNED sites — 3 identity +
rot90 + mirror (the D8 dictionary) — generated by the walk script.

    Arm D (template + 8-orientation refs, zero residuals):  z19 = 25,573
    Shipping pick on the same d8 fixture:                    z19 = 26,748
    → Arm D WINS by 1,175 B on the aligned-site class.

Capability: 5/5 refs discovered (3 identity + rot90 + fliplr) with exact
zero residual under the honest mode-2 semantics (residual = block −
T(template)).

The C-vs-D asymmetry is the finding: C's sites were planted at
non-block-aligned coordinates (my fixture-design offset), fragmenting
the predictor residuals around the planted region; D's aligned sites
make the whole planted region residual-zero under template subtraction.
Alignment is part of the shared-model grammar's contract — site
coordinates are transmitted, but the REFERENCE unit is the block.

## Arm E measured (2%-corrupted refs, residual-carrying) — FIRST genuine win

g3_shared_d8_2pct.png: the aligned D8 fixture with 2% pixel corruption
(±3 per channel).

    Arm E (refs with coded residuals):     z19 = 9,690 B
    Shipping pick on the same fixture:     z19 = 27,641 B
    → Arm E wins by ~17,951 (−65%), refs 5/5.

Residual accounting honest: 5 refs × (6 B header + 3,072 B ±3 sparse
residual payload) transmitted fully; the residuals' amplitude (not a
cheat) is what the container crushes. This is the class where
shared-model prediction DOMINATES both exact rules (they can't fire —
residual ≠ 0) and per-pixel prediction (full-price residuals): the
bridge Jon predicted.

Findings (the three sources separated):

   shipping pick on both real winners** — document 783 vs 923, poster

2. **D8's incremental value is signed per class**: on document it COSTS

3. **The corrupt fixture's identity arm LOSES to the pick (28,742 vs

## G4-revised gate (2026-10-28, the review's correction list implemented)

The external review's verdict was accepted: the in-session harness was
not the shipping portfolio. The revised gate
(crates/mnf-mans/scripts/g4_gate.py) replaces the internal 4-stream
baseline with the RELEASED 1.10.0 CLI's complete --mnf8 output
(verify-checked per file), runs the MISSING comparison pair under one
budget (identity+sparse vs D8+sparse, the transform axis toggled alone),
and meters each arm's time. Corpus: real sprite frames (the user's
named class), document, poster, screenshot (mixed-content control),
gradient/solid (negative controls).

Results (complete files; CLI baseline includes its own
portfolio+MNF9 arm):

| fixture | A = CLI 1.10.0 | id+sparse | D8+sparse | geometry's value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| guard-strip (real sprite frames) | 315 | 320 (n=1) | 320 (n=1) | 0 |
| hero-pair (identical) | 220 | **176** (n=1) | 176 | 0 |
| document | 923 | **793** (n=5) | 859 (n=10) | **−66 (geometry costs)** |
| poster | 1142 | **751** (n=5) | 751 | 0 |
| screenshot (mixed) | 678 | 817 (n=71) | 817 | 0 |
| gradient | 254 | no-fire | no-fire | — |
| solid | 36 | 284 | 284 | 0 |

Findings — the review's key question answered:

1. **Sparse residuals + template reuse are the mechanism; the D8 is
   not.** On every naturally structured fixture the identity-only arm
   equals or beats the D8 arm: document's five extra transformed
   references COST 66 B (D8's residual payloads outprice the identity
   arm's literals). Geometry's measured value remains confined to
   genuinely TRANSFORMED content (the planted rot90/fliplr fixtures:
   +2,311/+2,363 where identity cannot express the reference at all).
2. **The sprite hypothesis is tested on real sprite frames with an
   honest negative**: guard walk frames (46-164 px delta per frame) do
   NOT fire the arm — sprite edits are redrawn limbs (few pixels, full
   AMPLITUDE), so the sparse-residual payload pays full price; the
   sparse-corrupt win class is ±3-amplitude, not full-amplitude
   sparsity. hero-pair (pixel-identical frames) wins −44 via a plain
   shared template. The sprite promise is therefore restricted to
   exact/near-amplitude-delta frame families.
3. **Savings-rate (reported, never a promotion score)**: poster ≈
   283 kB/s of search, document ≈ 1.1 kB/s (the slow one), hero ≈
   218 kB/s — the arm's value-per-time is high where it wins, and the
   winning arms need ≤ ms-scale work at these sizes.

Decoder hardening checklist for the (future) format candidate —
carried here from the review: reject invalid modes, zero or forward
reference distances, out-of-range transform ids, truncated sections,
duplicate/out-of-range sparse positions, and unused trailing stream
bytes (the same discipline shipped in MNF9's decoder and the
consumption-check rule the Autopsy walker follows).

## G5: the sparse-representation economics (2026-10-28, Jon's directive)

The question: can approximate template reuse with compactly-coded
sparse corrections earn its own production representation? The three
competing representations (same frozen budget: one identity template
per file, same profitability boundary, z19+z9 containers, complete
serialized cost) measured per file against the RELEASED 1.10.0
portfolio, plus the mandatory 30-file corpus control:

| fixture | A = 1.10.0 | S1 pos+delta | S2 mask+packed | S3 channel-aware | best |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| document | 923 | **711** (n=3) | 755 | 780 | −212 |
| poster | 1142 | **751** (n=5) | 758 | 758 | −391 |
| hero-pair | 220 | **176** (n=1) | 195 | 202 | −44 |
| guard-strip | 315 | 352 | 362 | 369 | +37 |
| screenshot | 678 | 817 | **713** | 892 | +35 |
| solid | 36 | 284 | 169 | 184 | +133 |
| gradient/blob/plaid | — | no-fire | no-fire | no-fire | — |

Corpus control (all 30 files, all arms both T): fires 5/30, wins 2/30
(document −212 S1, poster −391 S1), 0 regressions (the pick = min over
arms keeps 1.10.0's candidates by construction).

Verdicts:

1. **S1 (explicit position + full RGB-delta payload) wins EVERYWHERE it
   matters** — S2's compressed masks and S3's channel-splitting cost
   more framing than they save on every fixture measured (the
   containers already compress the residual planes' run structure; the
   mask/pack framing is fixed overhead). The mask/channel hypotheses
   are DENIED on this corpus.
2. **The G4-class wins sharpened**: document −212 (G4's 793 came from a
   less-favorable sweep resolution; the T=32 sweep = fewer, bigger refs).
3. **The branch closes per the pre-registered rule**: the winning total
   is 603 B on 2/30 files. A new wire representation does not clear
   "earns its maintenance cost" on 2/30 files × a few hundred bytes —
   the identity+sparse arm rides G4's measured capability; its PATH to
   production is as a pick-arm inside a future release that bundles
   larger shared-structure work (or on a corpus where such classes
   dominate), not as a standalone grammar now.

The Acoustics-branch analogy (sparse corrections over shared frames)
should expect the same economics: the amplitude distribution decides,
the position-coding form barely matters (the container eats runs).
