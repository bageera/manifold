## Low-dose probe result (EMPIAR-11213 apoferritin, 553 frames) (2026-10-27)

Corpus: Falcon 4i at heavily fractionated dose (553 frames, wire 201 MiB =
382,502 B/frame — 1/10th of the 10943 high-dose file's per-frame wire).

| arm | bytes/frame | vs wire |
| --- | --- | --- |
| vendor EER wire | 382,502 | — |
| **event-delta-list (u32 dy + val, z3)** | **380,875** | **0.996×** |
| dense-render zlib-3 | 662,797 | 1.73× worse |
| frame-chain | 998,616 | 2.6× worse |
| Shannon H(p)-bound | ~235,213 | (p=0.0150, H=0.112 bits/px) |

**The density-threshold prediction is CONFIRMED on real data:** at
p=0.52 (high-dose render) dense-LZ beats event lists; at p=0.015
(low-dose) the event list ≈ the vendor wire and beats dense by 1.74×.
Shannon H(p) tracks both regimes (0.112 vs 1.0 bits/px). The frame-chain
arm loses everywhere (drift).

Verdict for the arms: the sparse-event mode is real in the low-dose regime
— but the vendor's own wire is already at the measured H-level (0.4%
within our delta-list's edge, ~noise). A MANIFOLD format item would need
to beat the vendor wire MATERIAL, not tie it; the honest verdict: the wire
is already near the entropy bound at this dose (0.11 bits/px), so the
compression opportunity in arm S is small and bounded. Candidate shelved
with evidence; arm S stays closed pending a genuinely novel structure
(discovery beyond H(p)).

The EER paper's claims all validated in both regimes (see
research-paper-validation.md).
